1. UNC Chapel Hill, Geography and Environment: GeoAI and Geospatial Data Science

Status: The posting was open when I re-read it on 26 November. It shows "Application Deadline 12/01/2026" and "Open Until Filled: No", so 1 December is a hard cutoff.

Why it ranks first this week: The ad asks for a scholar who "develops and applies cutting-edge geospatial computational methods", names machine and deep learning, and lists weather and climate change among its applied domains. Your dissertation and your first-author ACM TIST and SIGSPATIAL papers are in this exact area. The skeptical chair rated the file "likely" for the long list.

The risk: The minimum is a "PhD in Geography or a related field by the time of appointment". A Computer Science PhD in spatial data science very likely counts as related, but the ad does not say so. A geography committee will also ask how your methods answer geographic questions.

What it needs: A one-page cover letter, a CV, a two-page research statement, a two-page teaching statement, a one-page mentoring statement, and the names of four references. Letters are requested later. You have three letter writers on record, so you need a fourth name.

People: Dr. Paul Delamater is named in the ad as search committee chair. No tie to Prof. Shekhar or Prof. Kumar was found in the department.

Lead with: physics-informed and knowledge-guided GeoAI, framed as methods for climate, mobility and human-environment questions. The teaching statement should address the department's new B.S. in Geospatial Data Science.

Network: Vandana Janeja is PI and Jianwu Wang is co-PI of NSF 2118285, the iHARP institute, and Prof. Shekhar is a co-PI on the same award. I confirmed the award record myself. Method and open items

What was done

  • Triage: two independent readers judged all 151 rows on field and rank only, and a row survived if either reader kept it. 109 rows survived.
  • Bundles: 16 bundle rows were opened and split into 59 individual searches.
  • Verification: 48 searches from the sheet and 12 found searches each had the employer's posting read for rank, dates, PhD wording, sponsorship and letters, with a quote for each fact. A second agent mapped the chair, committee, area faculty and recent hires of each unit.
  • Roster: six agents built a list of 256 people tied to Prof. Shekhar, Prof. Kumar or you, from Prof. Shekhar's CV, Prof. Kumar's alumni page, dissertation records, OpenAlex and NSF award records. The 165 academics had their current post confirmed.
  • Adversarial checks: every portal was reopened by a different agent, 11 tie claims were challenged, and 23 files were read by a skeptical search chair. Three ties were downgraded and two searches were found closed.
  • My own checks: I re-read the postings for UNC Geography, UMBC, Ohio State and UT Austin, and the NSF record for award 2118285.
